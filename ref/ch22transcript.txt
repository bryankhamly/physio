## 22.1 An Overview of Reproductive Physiology

**Topics covered:**
- The role of gametes in sexual reproduction
- Gene sorting and packaging in gametogenesis: meiosis
- Components of the reproductive system
- Events following fertilization
- Patterns of reproductive activity over the human life span

---

### The Role of Gametes in Sexual Reproduction

**Gametes:**
- Male gametes = spermatozoa
- Female gametes = ova
- Haploid: n chromosomes
- Diploid: 2n chromosomes

**Gametes (haploid):**
- 23 chromosomes: 22 autosomes, 1 sex

**Fertilization → zygote (diploid):**
- 46 chromosomes: 44 autosomes, 2 sex

---

### Figure 22.1: The process of fertilization from undifferentiated stem cells to the zygote

**Process overview:**
- Male undifferentiated stem cells (Diploid)
- Female undifferentiated stem cells (Diploid)
- ↓ Gametogenesis
- Spermatozoa (n = 23) — Ovum (n = 23) [Haploid]
- ↓ Fertilization
- Zygote (Diploid, 2n = 46)

---

### Chromosomes

- Autosomes come in pairs: 22 pairs
- Each member of a pair carries the same complement of genes
- Sex chromosomes come in two forms: X and Y
- Not all genes are paired

**Sex chromosomes:**
- X chromosome is found in both males and females
  - Has essential genes
- Y chromosome is found only in males
  - Has genes essential for developing into male
- XX = female
- XY = male

---

### Gene Sorting and Packaging in Gametogenesis: Meiosis

**Production of gametes from undifferentiated germ cells:**

**Mitosis:** normal cell division
- DNA replicated once
- Cells undergo division once

**Meiosis:** cell division to produce gametes
- DNA replicated once
- Cells undergo division twice

---

### Figure 22.3: Meiosis

**Process:**
1. Diploid germ cell (2n chromosomes) with Maternal and Paternal chromosomes
2. DNA is replicated (2n × 2 chromosomes) → Sister chromatids form, connected at centromere
3. Homologous chromosomes group together in pairs
4. Crossing over occurs
5. New chromosomes containing mixtures of maternal and paternal genes are created
6. Independent assortment occurs
7. **Meiosis I** → (n × 2 chromosomes)
8. **Meiosis II** → (n chromosomes)
9. **Haploid daughter cells**

---

### Components of the Reproductive System

**Reproductive system:** all organs involved in mating, gametogenesis, or other functions involved in producing offspring
- Gonads
- Accessory reproductive organs

**Gonads:**
- Male: testes
- Female: ovaries

**Two major functions:**
1. Produce gametes
2. Secrete sex hormones

---

### Sex Hormones

- Testes: androgens
- Ovaries: estrogens and progesterone
- Although males have more androgens and females have more estrogens and progesterone, these steroids are found in both sexes
- Adrenal cortex also produces androgens

**Accessory reproductive organs:**
- Organs of the reproductive tract: transport of gametes
- Glands: secrete fluids into reproductive tract

---

### Events Following Fertilization

**Copulation:** act of mating
- Sperm deposited into female to fertilize ovum

**After fertilization: pregnancy or gestation**
- First two months: embryo
- After two months: fetus
- About nine months: parturition or birth

---

### Sex Determination

Chromosomes determine whether fetus develops ovaries or testes: sex determination

**Y chromosome: srY gene (sex-determining region of the Y chromosome)**
- If srY gene present: testes
- If srY gene absent: ovaries

**srY gene:**
- Codes for testis-determining factor
- If present: testis-determining factor causes fetus to develop testes
- If absent: ovaries develop

---

### Sex Differentiation

Sex differentiation controlled by hormones

**First two weeks:** embryo sexually indifferent
- Wolffian ducts (precursor to male tract)
- Müllerian ducts (precursor to female tract)

**If testes are present (due to srY gene), testes secrete hormones:**
- Testosterone: stimulates development of Wolffian ducts
- Müllerian-inhibiting hormone (MIH): degeneration of Müllerian ducts

**No testes:**
- Hormones absent → Wolffian ducts regress, Müllerian ducts develop

---

### Figure 22.4: Development of male and female internal reproductive organs during embryonic life

**Sexually indifferent stage:**
- Wolffian duct

- Primitive gonad

**Male development (7–8 week embryo):**
- Testis
- Vas deferens
- Degenerating Müllerian duct
- Seminal vesicle

**At birth (male):**
- Seminal vesicle
- Prostate gland
- Vas deferens
- Testis
- Penis
- Urethra

**Female development (8–9 week embryo):**
- Ovary
- Degenerating Wolffian duct
- Uterine tube

**At birth (female):**
- Uterine tube
- Ovary
- Uterus
- Vagina

---

### Figure 22.5: The roles of sex determination and sex differentiation

**Male pathway:**
- X + Y Egg/Sperm → Fertilization → XY Zygote
- Presence of srY gene → Sex determination → Testes
- Presence of testosterone → Wolffian ducts → Male reproductive structures
- Presence of MIS → Müllerian ducts → Regression

**Female pathway:**
- X + X Egg/Sperm → Fertilization → XX Zygote
- Absence of srY gene → Sex determination → Ovaries
- Absence of testosterone → Wolffian ducts → Regression
- Absence of MIS → Müllerian ducts → Female reproductive structures

---

### Patterns of Reproductive Activity Over the Human Life Span

**Adolescence (prior to 10–14 years):**
- Inability to reproduce

**Puberty (starts at 10–14 years):**
- Sexual maturation
- Reproductive organs mature (can produce gametes)
- Secondary sexual characteristics develop

**During sexual maturation (from late teens onward):**
- Female reproductive system cycles
- Female loses ability to reproduce at approximately age 45–50
- Menopause: loss of female reproductive capacity
- Male retains ability to produce sperm

### Sertoli Cells

**Functions:**
- Support sperm development
- Secrete lumenal fluid in which sperm develop
- Secrete androgen-binding protein
  - Androgen buffer that helps maintain steady level of androgens in lumen
- Target cells to testosterone and FSH
- Secrete chemicals that stimulate spermatogenesis
- Secrete inhibin
  - Hormone of negative feedback for FSH
- Secrete MIH

---

### External Genitalia

**Penis:**
- Male copulatory organ
- Penetrates vagina and deposits sperm
- At rest, penis is flaccid
- During sexual arousal, penis undergoes erection
  - Blood flow to penis increases
  - Blood engorges erectile tissue
  - Penis swells and elongates
- Ejaculation
  - Sperm is ejected through urethra

**Scrotum:**
- Suspended beneath penis
- Houses testes
- Sperm cannot develop at body temperature
- Allows a cooler environment for sperm development

---

### Sperm Transport Pathway

Seminiferous tubules → Rete testis → Efferent ductules → Exit testis → Epididymis → Vas deferens

---

### Accessory Glands

**Seminal vesicles:**
- Secrete alkaline fluid with fructose, enzymes, and prostaglandins

**Prostate gland:**
- Secrete citrate and enzymes

**Bulbourethral glands:**
- Secrete viscous fluid with mucus

---

### Hormonal Regulation of Reproductive Function in Males

**Gonadotropin releasing hormone (GnRH):**
- From hypothalamus

**Gonadotropins:**
- FSH: stimulates gametogenesis and inhibin
- LH: stimulates androgen secretion

**Blood levels of sex hormones in males:**
- Pulsatile
- Fairly constant on a short-term basis
- Prepubescent → low blood levels
- Puberty and adulthood → high blood levels

---

### Figure 22.8: Hormonal regulation of reproductive function in males

**Pathway:**
1. Hypothalamus → GnRH secretion
2. Anterior pituitary → FSH secretion + LH secretion
3. LH → Leydig cells → Testosterone secretion → Plasma testosterone
4. Testosterone provides Negative feedback to hypothalamus
5. FSH → Sertoli cells → Inhibin secretion + Spermatogenesis
6. Inhibin provides Negative feedback (FSH only) to anterior pituitary

---

### Sperm and Their Development

**Sperm anatomy:**
- **Head**
  - Chromosomes
  - Acrosome: Enzymes necessary for fertilization
- **Midpiece**
  - Mitochondria
- **Tail**
  - Whiplike movements propel sperm

---

### Figure 22.10: Spermatogenesis

**Process (from basement membrane to lumen):**
1. Spermatogonia (2n)
2. ↓ Mitosis and differentiation
3. Primary spermatocytes (2n × 2)
4. ↓ Meiosis I
5. Secondary spermatocytes (n × 2)
6. ↓ Meiosis II
7. Spermatids (n)
8. ↓ Differentiation
9. Spermatozoa (n)

**Notes:**
- Process occurs from basement membrane to lumen
- Following mitosis, one spermatogonium stays to produce more
- Sertoli cells and tight junctions support the process

---

### Sperm Maturation

- Spermatozoa released into lumen of seminiferous tubules
- Remain immotile for 20 days
- Move to epididymis by peristalsis and flow of lumenal fluid
- Acquire motility
- Move to vas deferens by peristalsis
- Remain in vas deferens until ejaculation

---

### Sexual Response in Males

**Controlled by autonomic nervous system:**
- Spinal reflexes

**Phases:**
1. Erection: parasympathetic nervous system
2. Emission: sympathetic nervous system
3. Ejaculation: sympathetic nervous system

**Stimuli:**
- Mechanoreceptors of penis and erogenous zones
- Descending signals from higher brain centers

---

### Figure 22.11: Events leading to erection

**Pathway:**
1. Mechanical stimulation of receptors in penis + Activity in descending neural pathways from brain
2. ↓ Spinal cord
3. Activity in sympathetic neurons to penis ↓ + Activity in parasympathetic neurons to penis ↑
4. Arterioles supplying blood to erectile tissue → Relaxation of smooth muscle
5. Erectile tissue: Blood flow ↑ → Blood volume and blood pressure ↑
6. Compression of veins → Outflow of blood ↓
7. **Erection** (Positive feedback loop)

---

### Emission

- Response to mechanical stimulation continues from erection to emission
- Neural activity shifts from parasympathetic to sympathetic control
- Contractions of epididymis, vas deferens, and ejaculatory duct
- Secretions from seminal vesicles and prostate
- Result: movement of semen (sperm and fluid) into urethra

---

### Ejaculation

- Response to mechanical stimulation continues to ejaculation
- Mediated by sympathetic activity
- Stimulates contractions of smooth muscle in urethra and skeletal muscle at base of penis
- Stimulates closure of urethral sphincter
- Result: movement of semen through urethra and out of body

### Ova and Their Development

**Oogenesis:** oogonia → ova

- Females: number of oogonia is fixed prior to birth

- Meiosis begins in fetal life

---

### Figure 22.14: Oogenesis, the development of ova

5. ↓ Meiosis I

**AFTER FERTILIZATION:**
7. ↓ Meiosis II

### The Sexual Response in Females

- Vascular congestion of clitoris
- Vaginal secretions
- Nipple erection
- Orgasm
  - Rhythmic contractions of vagina and uterus
  - Increased blood pressure and heart rate
  - Widespread skeletal muscle contractions

---

### The Menstrual Cycle

Repeated changes in the ovary and uterus through time

**Includes two subcycles:**
1. Ovarian cycle
2. Uterine cycle

Caused by changes in estrogen and progesterone concentrations

---

### Ovarian Cycle

**Follicular phase:**
- Menstruation → ovulation
- Lasts approximately 14 days

**Luteal phase:**
- Ovulation → before menstruation
- Lasts approximately 14 days

---

### Follicular Phase

- FSH and estrogens stimulate follicle growth and development
- A few follicles develop from primordial follicles
- Oocyte grows; granulosa cells proliferate
- Zona pellucida and antrum form
- Dominant follicle continues development; remaining follicles regress
- Corona radiata develops
- Graafian follicle = mature follicle
- Ovulation

**Late follicular phase:**
- GnRH surge causes LH surge
- LH surge triggers ovulation and development of corpus luteum in luteal phase
- Ovulation: wall of Graafian follicle ruptures
- Antral fluid with oocyte flows to ovary surface
- Oocyte is released and enters fimbriae

---

### Luteal Phase

- Ruptured follicle → gland = corpus luteum
- Corpus luteum secretes estrogens and progesterone
- Corpus luteum reaches maximum activity in 10 days, then degenerates (luteolysis)
- Luteolysis → decreased estrogens and progesterone → menstruation (end of luteal phase)
- If oocyte is fertilized, degeneration does not occur

---

### Figure 22.15a: The ovarian cycle

**Follicle development:**
- Primordial follicle → Primary follicle → Secondary follicle (with Zona pellucida, Antrum, Oocyte, Granulosa cells, Follicle cells, Theca cells)
- → Graafian follicle (with Cumulus oophorus, Oocyte, Corona radiata, Antrum)
- → Ovulation → Ruptured follicle → Corpus luteum
- → Degenerating corpus luteum → Corpus albicans

---

### Fraternal Twins

- Two or more follicles may become dominant and released at ovulation
- If both are fertilized, fraternal twins result
- Also called dizygotic twins (develop from two zygotes)

---

### Uterine Cycle

| Phase | Days | Ovarian Phase |
|-------|------|---------------|
| Menstrual phase | days 1–5 | Follicular phase |
| Proliferative phase | menstruation–day 14 | Follicular phase |
| Secretory phase | days 15–28 | Luteal phase |

---

### Menstrual Phase

- Shedding of uterine lining
- Blood flow to tissue decreases
- Tissues die and slough into vagina, causing menstrual flow

**Hormonal control of menstruation:**
- Triggered by decreased levels of estrogens and progesterone
- Hormone levels decrease when corpus luteum degenerates

---

### Proliferative Phase

- Uterus prepares for fertilized ovum
- Endometrial lining develops
- Endometrial layer grows
- Endometrial glands enlarge
- Smooth muscle layer thickens
- Cervical glands secrete mucus

**Hormonal control of proliferative phase:**
- Estrogens stimulate development of uterine lining

---

### Secretory Phase

- Endometrium prepared for implantation
- Blood supply increased
- Glands enlarge and secrete glycogen-rich fluids
- Cervical secretions are more sticky, forming a plug

---

### Figure 22.16: The uterine cycle

**Structure:**
- Days 1–5: Menstrual phase (menstruation)
- Days 5–14: Proliferative phase
- Days 14–28: Secretory phase
- Blood vessels and Endometrial glands develop
- Endometrial thickness increases through proliferative and secretory phases

---

### Hormonal Changes During Menstrual Cycle

- Estrogen is first secreted from follicles, then from the corpus luteum
- Progesterone is secreted from corpus luteum
- LH and FSH are secreted from anterior pituitary gland
- Estrogens and progesterone inhibit LH and FSH secretion

---

### Hormonal Changes in the Early to Mid-Follicular Phase

- Short-lived declines in plasma estrogen and progesterone (final stages of luteolysis)
- LH and FSH consequently increase slightly
- FSH binds to receptors on granulosa cells, causing follicles to grow
- FSH causes the outer granulosa layer to differentiate into thecal cells
- LH acts on thecal LH receptors to stimulate testosterone synthesis
- Testosterone is converted by granulosa cells into estrogens
- Estrogen concentrations increase: negative feedback on GnRH and LH and FSH
- LH and FSH levels remain constant but estrogens continue to rise due to proliferating cells and increased LH and FSH receptors

---

### Figure 22.17: Summary of hormonal changes in the menstrual cycle

**Hormone patterns:**
- **Estrogen:** rises through follicular phase, peaks before ovulation, rises again in luteal phase
- **Progesterone:** low in follicular phase, rises significantly in luteal phase
- **LH:** relatively stable, with major surge at ovulation
- **FSH:** relatively stable, slight rise at beginning, small surge at ovulation

---

### Figure 22.18: Regulation of hormone secretion during the early to mid-follicular phase

**Pathway:**
1. Hypothalamus → GnRH secretion
2. Anterior pituitary → FSH secretion + LH secretion
3. LH → Theca cells → Androgen secretion
4. Androgens → Granulosa cells → Enzymatic modification of androgens → Estrogen secretion → Plasma estrogen
5. Estrogen provides Negative feedback to hypothalamus
6. FSH → Granulosa cells → Inhibin secretion → Negative feedback (FSH only)
7. Result: Oogenesis and follicular development

---

### Hormonal Changes in the Late Follicular Phase

- Estrogens switch from negative feedback to positive feedback in hypothalamus; mechanism is unknown
- LH levels rise, leading to more estrogen and, consequently, more LH
- LH surge

**Changes induced in dominant follicle under influence of LH surge:**
- Meiosis I completed
- Estrogen declines
- Progesterone production by granulosa cells initiated
- Enzymes secreted, breaking down follicular wall
- Follicular wall ruptures 18 hours after surge peak
- Granulosa and thecal cells differentiate into luteal cells

---

### Figure 22.19: Regulation of hormone secretion during the late follicular phase

**Pathway:**
1. Hypothalamus → GnRH secretion
2. Anterior pituitary → LH (and FSH) secretion
3. LH → Granulosa cells → Estrogen secretion
4. Estrogen provides **Positive feedback** to hypothalamus
5. Result: LH surge → Ovulation

---

### Figure 22.20: Regulation of hormone secretion during the luteal phase

**Pathway:**
1. Hypothalamus → GnRH secretion
2. Anterior pituitary → FSH secretion + LH secretion
3. LH/FSH → Corpus luteum → Estrogen secretion + Progesterone secretion + Inhibin secretion
4. Estrogen and Progesterone provide Negative feedback to hypothalamus
5. Inhibin provides Negative feedback (FSH only) to anterior pituitary
6. Result: Plasma progesterone ↑, Plasma estrogen ↑, Plasma inhibin ↑

---

### Hormonal Changes in the Luteal Phase

- Estrogen levels continue to decline
- LH surge is terminated
- Progesterone from luteal cells increases
  - Promotes uterine development and preparation for pregnancy
  - Powerful inhibitor of GnRH and LH/FSH secretion
- Estrogen also increases but its potential stimulatory effects are blocked by progesterone
- After 10 days in absence of implantation, corpus luteum starts to degenerate (spontaneous in humans)
- Progesterone and estrogen decline, triggering menstruation

---

### Long-Term Regulation of Female Reproductive Function

**Puberty:**
Estrogen produces secondary sex characteristics:
- Widening of hips
- Deposition of fat in breasts, hips, and buttocks
- Skin oil
- Body hair changes
- Growth of bones

**Reproductive years:**
- Estrogen levels remain high to maintain secondary sex characteristics

**Menopause:**
- Estrogen levels decrease
- Reversal of some secondary sex characteristics
- Hot flashes
- Increased risk of heart disease and osteoporosis

---

## 22.4 Fertilization, Implantation, and Pregnancy

- Fertilization usually takes place in uterine tube
- Sperm must be introduced into female reproductive tract within five days before ovulation
- Sperm remain viable for 5 days
- Oocyte remains viable for 12–24 hours
- Sperm are initially incapable of fertilization
  - Requires capacitation

---

### Ovum Transport

- At ovulation, fluid movement causes the oocyte to enter the fimbriae of the uterine tube
- Peristaltic contractions move the oocyte toward the uterus for several minutes
- Activity of cilia move the oocyte toward the uterus for a few days
- Entire trip takes four days
- Sperm must meet ovum during these four days

---

### Events of Fertilization

- Millions of sperm are deposited into vagina during copulation
- Sperm swim to the uterus and uterine tube
- Survival time in reproductive tract is approximately five days
- Only a few hundred sperm reach the uterine tubes

**Sperm capacitation:**
- Deposited sperm cannot fertilize oocyte
- Capacitation occurs in female reproductive tract
- Tail movement
- Plasma membrane altered

---

### Fertilization Process

1. Several sperm generally reach the ovum and try penetrating the corona radiata
2. When they get through the corona radiata, sperm bind to sperm-binding protein
3. Acrosome reaction is triggered, with acrosomal enzymes released
4. Enzymes break through the zona pellucida, allowing sperm to access the oocyte
5. The first sperm to access the oocyte binds to a receptor on the oocyte plasma membrane
6. Sperm transported into cytoplasm = fusion
7. Sperm-binding proteins become inactivated and the zona pellucida hardens, preventing polyspermy
8. Fusion stimulates second meiotic division of the oocyte
9. Oocyte → ovum
10. Sperm plasma membrane disintegrates
11. Chromosomes from sperm and ovum migrate to center
12. DNA replicated → zygote

---

### Early Embryonic Development and Implantation

**Mitotic divisions → morula:**
- Cell cleavage (no increase in overall size)
- Totipotent until the 16- to 32-cell stage

**Blastocyst:**
- Lost zona pellucida
- **Outer cell layer: trophoblast**
  - Will become fetal placenta
- **Inner cell mass**
  - Will become embryo
- **Fluid-filled cavity: blastocele**

---

### Figure 22.22a: Early embryonic development and implantation

**Timeline:**
1. Ovulation → Ovary
2. Fertilization → Zygote (fertilized ovum) with Zona pellucida
3. Early cleavage (4-cell stage)
4. Morula (3–4 days postfertilization)
5. Blastocyst (4–5 days postfertilization)
   - Inner cell mass
   - Trophoblast

### Implantation

- Occurs 6–7 days post fertilization
- Trophoblast is responsible for implantation
- Secretes enzymes that digest endometrial cells to provide nourishment for the embryo
- Secretes paracrines that stimulate the decidual response
- Infiltrates endometrial tissue to develop into the placenta

**Process:**
- Blastocyst attaches to endometrial lining
- Endometrium swells, increases glycogen stores
- Trophoblast penetrates endometrium
- Trophoblast and endometrial cells will form placenta

---

- Endometrium

---

### Hormonal Changes During Pregnancy

**hCG secreted from placenta:**
- Sustains corpus luteum
- Corpus luteum secretes estrogens and progesterone during first two months of pregnancy
- Placenta secretes estrogens and progesterone during remainder of pregnancy
- Progesterone inhibits GnRH, LH, and FSH release, preventing LH surge

---

### Effects of Pregnancy Hormones

**Estrogen:**
- Growth of duct tissue in the breasts
- Prolactin secretion
- Growth and enhanced contractile responsiveness of smooth muscle in uterus
- Oxytocin

**Progesterone:**
- Growth of glandular tissue in the breasts
- Maintenance of secretory-phase uterine conditions
- Suppression of contractile activity of smooth muscle in uterus

- **Human chorionic gonadotropin:** peaks around month 2-3, then declines
- **Estrogens:** gradually increases throughout pregnancy, highest at parturition
- **Progesterone:** gradually increases throughout pregnancy, highest at parturition

## 22.5 Parturition and Lactation

- **Pregnancy:** 40 weeks
- **Parturition:** birth
- **Lactation:** milk production in mammary glands

---

### Events of Parturition

**End of pregnancy:**
- Cervix ripens

**Beginning of parturition:**
- Cervix dilates
- Labor (uterine contractions)

**Parturition:**
- Baby's head wedges cervix open
- Baby is born head first
- Expulsion of placenta: afterbirth

---

### Figure 22.26: Events of parturition

**Structures involved:**
- Placenta
- Umbilical cord
- Uterus
- Cervix (closed → dilates)
- Vagina

---

### Mechanism of Parturition

**Positive feedback loop:**
1. Weak uterine contractions
2. Pressure of fetus against cervix
3. ↓
4. Oxytocin secretion from posterior pituitary
5. ↓
6. Strengthen uterine contractions
7. ↓
8. **Parturition**

---

### Trigger for Parturition

Trigger for parturition is unknown, but current theory suggests signal comes from the fetus

**Fetal placenta pathway:**
- CRH → fetal ACTH → DHEA
- DHEA → estrogens
- Enters maternal bloodstream
- Estrogen → parturition

---

### Lactation

**Inhibition of lactation:**
- Estrogen and progesterone

**Stimulation of lactation:**
- Prolactin stimulates milk synthesis
- Oxytocin stimulates the milk ejection reflex

**Colostrum:**
- Watery milk produced in the first few days after birth
- Contains proteins, but few nutrients

**Later milk:**
- Nutrients, growth factors, hormones, and antibodies
