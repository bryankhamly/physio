Chapter 20

The Gastrointestinal System

Digestion, absorption, secretion, and motility

1

Overview

The Four GI Processes

  • Digestion: Break down food into absorbable particles
  • Absorption: Move nutrients from lumen → blood/lymph
  • Secretion: Release enzymes, mucus, acid, bile into lumen
  • Motility: Move contents through tract

Why Digestion?

Nutrients are too large to cross the epithelium. Must be broken down:

Starch→Glucose Proteins→Amino acids Triglycerides→Fatty acids + Monoglycerides
2

GI Anatomy

GI Tract Length
~15 feet (hollow tube)
Small Intestine
8-10 feet long

GI Tract Path

Mouth→ Pharynx→ Esophagus→ Stomach→ Small Intestine→ Colon→ Rectum

Four Layers of GI Wall (Lumen → Outside)

  1. Mucosa: Epithelium + Lamina propria (connective tissue with blood/lymph vessels, nerves, lymphoid tissue) + Muscularis mucosae (thin smooth muscle for mixing)
  2. Submucosa: Thick connective tissue, large blood/lymph vessels, Submucosal plexus (Meissner's)
  3. Muscularis externa: Inner circular (changes diameter) + Outer longitudinal (changes length), Myenteric plexus (Auerbach's) between layers
  4. Serosa: Inner connective tissue + Outer mesothelium (continuous with mesenteries and peritoneum)

Mucosa Cell Types (Enterocytes)

  • Absorptive cells: Absorb nutrients
  • Exocrine cells: Secrete enzymes, mucus
  • Endocrine cells: Secrete hormones

Enteric Nervous System ("Second Brain")

  • Submucosal plexus (Meissner's): Controls secretion & blood flow
  • Myenteric plexus (Auerbach's): Controls motility

~100 million neurons - can function independently of CNS! Communicates with ANS.

Mouth & Pharynx

  • Mouth: Digestion begins with mastication (chewing)
  • Saliva: Lubricant + salivary amylase (digests starch/glycogen)
  • Pharynx: Throat - common passageway for air and food

Esophagus

  • Muscular tube from pharynx to stomach
  • Upper 1/3: Skeletal muscle
  • Lower 2/3: Smooth muscle
  • Upper esophageal sphincter: Skeletal muscle (pharynx-esophagus)
  • Lower esophageal sphincter: Smooth muscle (esophagus-stomach)

Stomach Anatomy

  • Regions: Fundus → Body → Antrum → Pylorus
  • Volume: Empty = 50 mL | Full = 1000 mL
  • Rugae: Folds that flatten when stomach expands
  • Gastric pits: Lead to gastric glands
  • Pyloric sphincter: Controls gastric emptying

Stomach Cell Types

  • Parietal cells: Secrete HCl and intrinsic factor
  • Chief cells: Secrete pepsinogen
  • Neck cells: Secrete mucus
  • G cells: Secrete gastrin (hormone)

Stomach Acid (pH = 2)

  • Activates pepsinogen → pepsin
  • Denatures proteins
  • Kills bacteria
  • Gastric mucosal barrier: Protective layer of mucus + bicarbonate (from neck cells & goblet cells)

Small Intestine

Duodenum
Receives chyme, bile, pancreatic juice → Jejunum
Major nutrient absorption → Ileum
B12 & bile salt absorption

Key fact: Absorption completed within first 20% of intestine length!

Small Intestine Surface Area Adaptations

  • Villi: Finger-like projections containing blood vessels + lacteal (lymph vessel for fat absorption)
  • Microvilli: Form the brush border on apical membrane
  • Crypts of Lieberkuhn: Secrete bicarbonate-rich fluid; new epithelial cells arise here
Surface Area Structures
Rugae → Plicae → Villi → Microvilli
GALT
80% of all lymphocytes!

Hepatic Portal System

  • Nutrients absorbed into mesenteric veins
  • Travel via hepatic portal vein to liver
  • Liver to heart via hepatic vein
  • Hepatic artery supplies oxygen to liver
  • Nutrients go to liver FIRST before general circulation!

Large Intestine

  • Consists of: Cecum → Colon → Rectum
  • Ileocecal sphincter: Between ileum and cecum
  • Teniae coli: Bands of longitudinal smooth muscle
  • Vermiform appendix: At cecum

Four Sections of Colon

Ascending→ Transverse→ Descending→ Sigmoid

Functions: Concentrate waste into feces, absorb water, store feces until defecation

Rectum & Anus

  • Internal anal sphincter: Smooth muscle (involuntary)
  • External anal sphincter: Skeletal muscle (voluntary)
  • Both sphincters must relax for defecation

Key Structures

  • Mesenteries: Peritoneal sheets that hold intestines in place
  • Pylorus: Sphincter controlling stomach emptying into duodenum
  • Chyme: Processed stomach contents (food + gastric juice)

Accessory Glands

Overview

Glands outside the GI tract that secrete fluids and enzymes into the lumen via ducts.

  • Salivary glands
  • Pancreas
  • Liver
  • Gallbladder

Salivary Glands (3 Major Pairs)

  • Parotid gland - largest
  • Submandibular gland
  • Sublingual gland

Saliva Composition

  • Rich in bicarbonate ions
  • Contains mucus (lubrication)
  • Salivary amylase - digests starch
  • Lysozyme - antibacterial enzyme

Pancreas (Dual Function)

  • Exocrine: Produces pancreatic juice (acini)
  • Endocrine: Secretes hormones (Islets of Langerhans)

Key fact: Pancreas secretes more protein (enzymes) relative to weight than any other tissue!

Pancreatic Juice

  • Bicarbonate: From duct cells - neutralizes acidic chyme
  • Enzymes from acinar cells:
    • Pancreatic amylase & lipases
    • Proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase)
    • Nucleases

Pancreatic Duct System

  • Pancreatic duct joins common bile duct
  • Forms Ampulla of Vater
  • Sphincter of Oddi: Regulates flow from pancreas and gallbladder to duodenum

Liver Functions

  • Secretes bile (for fat digestion)
  • Processes nutrients:
    • Glucose → Glycogen (storage)
    • Amino acids → Fatty acids
    • Synthesizes triglycerides & cholesterol
    • Synthesizes lipoproteins
  • Detoxifies substances
  • Removes old RBCs: Catabolizes hemoglobin → Bilirubin
  • Synthesizes plasma proteins
  • Secretes and modifies hormones

Biliary System

  • Hepatocytes take up materials from blood in sinusoids
  • Secrete bile into bile canaliculi
  • Canaliculi drain into bile ducts
  • Bile ducts → Common hepatic duct
  • Common hepatic duct + Cystic duct = Common bile duct

Gallbladder

  • Stores and concentrates bile
  • Releases bile when stimulated by CCK
  • Connected to common bile duct via cystic duct

Carbohydrate Digestion

Typical Intake
~500 grams/day
Only Absorbed As
Monosaccharides

Carbohydrate Types

  • Disaccharides: Sucrose, Lactose, Maltose
  • Polysaccharides: Starch, Glycogen
  • Cellulose (fiber): CANNOT be digested - no human enzyme

Digestion Steps

  1. Mouth: Salivary amylase → Starch to maltose + limit dextrins
  2. Stomach: Amylase inactivated by acid (no carb digestion)
  3. Small intestine: Pancreatic amylase → maltose + limit dextrins
  4. Brush border enzymes: Final digestion to monosaccharides
Brush Border EnzymeSubstrateProducts
MaltaseMaltose2 Glucose
SucraseSucroseGlucose + Fructose
LactaseLactoseGlucose + Galactose
DextrinaseLimit dextrinsGlucose
GlucoamylasePolysaccharidesGlucose

Absorption Mechanisms

  • Glucose & Galactose:
    • Apical: SGLT1 (Na+-linked secondary active transport)
    • Basolateral: GLUT2 (facilitated diffusion)
  • Fructose:
    • Apical & Basolateral: Facilitated diffusion (GLUT5 apical, GLUT2 basolateral)

Lactose Intolerance

Lactase deficiency → undigested lactose fermented by bacteria → gas, bloating, diarrhea

Protein Digestion

Typical Intake
~125 grams/day
Products
Amino acids, dipeptides, tripeptides

Protein Sources in GI Tract

  • Consumed in diet
  • Secreted into lumen (enzymes)
  • Sloughed off from cells lining the tract

Zymogens (Inactive Enzyme Forms)

  • Inactive storage form of proteases
  • Stored in zymogen granules
  • Secreted by exocytosis
  • Activated by proteolysis (cleavage)

Digestion in Stomach

  • Chief cells secrete pepsinogen (inactive)
  • Parietal cells secrete HCl
  • HCl cleaves pepsinogen → pepsin (active)
  • Pepsin digests proteins into smaller peptide fragments

Protease Activation Cascade (Small Intestine)

Enterokinase
(brush border) → Trypsinogen → Trypsin → Activates all other proteases
  • Trypsin activates: Chymotrypsinogen → Chymotrypsin
  • Trypsin activates: Procarboxypeptidase → Carboxypeptidase
TypeEnzymesAction
EndopeptidasesPepsin, Trypsin, ChymotrypsinSplit peptides at interior bonds
ExopeptidasesCarboxypeptidase, AminopeptidaseCleave amino acids from ends

Absorption

  • Amino acids:
    • Apical: Na+-linked secondary active transport OR facilitated diffusion
    • Basolateral: Facilitated diffusion
  • Dipeptides & Tripeptides:
    • Apical: H+-peptide cotransporter (active transport)
    • Broken down inside cells to amino acids
    • Basolateral: Facilitated diffusion as amino acids

Fat Digestion

Typical Intake
~50 grams/day (90% triglycerides)
Special Problem
Not water soluble!

The Fat Digestion Challenge

  • Lipids are not water soluble
  • Do not mix with stomach or intestinal contents
  • Form fat droplets
  • Lipases can only act on molecules near the edge of a fat droplet
  • Solution: Emulsification by bile salts

Bile Salts

  • Synthesized in liver from cholesterol
  • Secreted in bile to duodenum
  • Amphipathic molecule: Has hydrophobic AND hydrophilic sides
  • Breaks large fat droplets into many small droplets = Emulsification
  • Increases surface area for lipase action

Digestion Steps

  1. Emulsification: Bile salts coat fat droplets → smaller droplets
  2. Digestion: Pancreatic lipases → Triglycerides become monoglycerides + fatty acids
  3. Micelles: Bile salts + digestion products form micelles
  4. Absorption: Free fatty acids & monoglycerides diffuse from micelles into enterocytes

Micelles

  • Small aggregates of bile salts, fatty acids, and monoglycerides
  • Equilibrium exists between micelles and free fatty acids/monoglycerides
  • Free forms can be absorbed by simple diffusion across epithelium
  • Micelles transport products to brush border

Fat Absorption Pathway (Inside Enterocyte)

Fatty acids + Monoglycerides enter cell → Enter Smooth ER
Re-form triglycerides → Golgi
Package into chylomicrons → Exocytosis to interstitial fluid → Enter LACTEAL (lymph)

Key: Fats go to LYMPH, NOT blood directly!

Chylomicrons

  • Large lipoprotein particles
  • Contain triglycerides, cholesterol, phospholipids, proteins
  • Too large to enter blood capillaries directly
  • Enter lacteals → lymph → general circulation

Enterohepatic Circulation

Liver → Bile salts → Gallbladder (storage) → Duodenum → Ileum (reabsorbed) → Hepatic portal vein → Back to Liver

Bile salts are recycled! ~95% reabsorbed in ileum.

Steatorrhea

Fat malabsorption → fatty stool. Causes: gallstones, pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis. Also causes fat-soluble vitamin (A, D, E, K) deficiency.

Vitamins & Minerals Absorption

Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)

  • Absorbed with lipids
  • Dissolve in fat droplets, micelles, and chylomicrons
  • Enter lymph system with fats

Water-Soluble Vitamins

  • Require special transport proteins
  • Absorbed by various mechanisms in small intestine

Vitamin B12 Absorption

  • MUST be bound to Intrinsic Factor (from parietal cells)
  • Absorbed only in ileum
  • Without IF → Pernicious anemia

Sodium (Na+)

  • Solvent drag: Follows water reabsorption passively
  • Actively absorbed in jejunum, ileum, and colon
  • Drives absorption of glucose, amino acids, and other solutes

Chloride (Cl-)

  • Passively follows sodium absorption
  • Maintains electrical neutrality

Potassium (K+)

  • Passively absorbed in small intestine
  • Secreted when lumenal concentrations are very low

Bicarbonate (HCO3-)

  • Jejunum: Passively absorbed
  • Ileum & Colon: Secreted in exchange for Cl- ions

Calcium (Ca2+)

  • Actively absorbed in duodenum and jejunum
  • Binds to calcium-binding protein on brush border
  • Transported into cells, then out via Ca2+ pump (basolateral)
  • 1,25-(OH)2D3 (Vitamin D) increases absorption by increasing calcium-binding protein

Iron (Fe)

  • Transferrin secreted by enterocytes into lumen
  • Transferrin binds iron
  • Transferrin-iron complex binds receptor
  • Taken into cells by receptor-mediated endocytosis
  • Some iron stored in enterocytes as ferritin
  • Some transported to blood bound to transferrin

Water Absorption

  • Secretions: ~7 liters/day
  • Intake: ~2 liters/day
  • Total: ~9 liters handled by GI tract daily!
  • Water absorption is passive
  • Water follows absorption of solutes by osmosis

GI Regulation

Key Principle

GI regulation is NOT homeostatic!

  • GI function maximizes absorption regardless of whether nutrients are needed
  • Conditions in the lumen are regulated to maximize absorption

Neural Control Pathways

  • Enteric Nervous System (ENS):
    • Submucosal plexus (Meissner's)
    • Myenteric plexus (Auerbach's)
  • CNS contribution: Through ANS communication with ENS

Sensory Receptors in GI Tract

  • Chemoreceptors: Detect chemical composition
  • Mechanoreceptors: Detect stretch/distension
  • Osmoreceptors: Detect osmolarity

These detect lumenal environment to initiate reflexes

PhaseStimulus OriginMechanism
CephalicHead (sight, smell, taste, thoughts)Requires CNS input (long reflexes)
GastricStomach (proteins, distension)Long & short reflexes + GI hormones
IntestinalSmall intestine (chyme)Long & short reflexes + GI hormones

Autonomic Control

  • Parasympathetic (vagus): ↑ motility, ↑ secretion, relaxes sphincters (excitatory)
  • Sympathetic: ↓ motility, ↓ secretion, contracts sphincters (inhibitory)
Short Reflexes
Within ENS only
Long Reflexes
Involve CNS (includes cephalic & emotional)

Food Intake Regulation

Long-Term Regulation: Leptin

  • Hormone released from adipose cells when calories exceed demands
  • Satiety signal - suppresses hunger
  • Increases metabolism

Leptin Pathway

Leptin from adipose → Arcuate nucleus (Hypothalamus) → Stimulates MSH + CART
  • MSH (melanocyte-stimulating hormone) + CART (cocaine- and amphetamine-related transcript)
  • ↑ Sympathetic activity
  • Stimulate release of TSH and ACTH
  • TSH & ACTH → ↑ metabolic rate, ↓ fat storage

Orexigenic Factors (Promote Eating)

  • NPY (Neuropeptide Y)
  • AgRP (Agouti-related peptide)

Effects:

  • ↑ Appetite (promote eating)
  • ↓ Metabolism
  • ↑ Parasympathetic activity
  • ↓ Sympathetic activity
  • Inhibit TSH and ACTH secretion

Short-Term Regulation: Hunger vs Satiety

Satiety FactorsOrexigenic Factors
InsulinGhrelin
CCK
Neural input from mechanoreceptors & chemoreceptors

Ghrelin - The "Hunger Hormone"

  • Released from stomach when empty
  • Stimulates appetite
  • Opposes leptin's effects

GI Hormones

GI Hormones Overview

Secreted from endocrine cells in the stomach and small intestine. Travel through blood to reach target organs.

CCK & Secretin Potentiation

  • CCK: Stimulates enzyme release; small bicarbonate effect; potentiates secretin
  • Secretin: Stimulates bicarbonate release; small enzyme effect; potentiates CCK
  • They enhance each other's effects!

Important Cells

  • Parietal cells: HCl + Intrinsic factor
  • Chief cells: Pepsinogen
  • G cells: Gastrin
  • ECL cells: Histamine (paracrine - stimulates HCl)
  • D cells: Somatostatin (inhibits GI hormones)

Hormone-Stimulus Pairs (Know These!)

  • Gastrin - peptides/amino acids (CORRECT)
  • CCK - fatty foods / amino acids (CORRECT)
  • Secretin - acid in small intestine (CORRECT)
  • GIP - glucose in small intestine (CORRECT)
  • Motilin - acid in stomach (WRONG! Motilin = fasting)

Secretion

Saliva Secretion

  • Stimulated by: Taste, texture (mechanoreceptors & chemoreceptors in mouth)
  • Relayed to salivary center of medulla
  • Sight and smell input from cerebral cortex also affects salivary center

Autonomic Control of Saliva

  • Parasympathetic: Watery saliva (primary control)
  • Sympathetic: Protein-rich, viscous saliva

Gastric Acid Secretion Mechanism

  1. Carbonic anhydrase in parietal cells: CO2 + H2O → H2CO3 → H+ + HCO3-
  2. H+ actively secreted into lumen (H+/K+ ATPase pump)
  3. HCO3- exchanged for Cl- at basolateral membrane
  4. Cl- diffuses into lumen through channels
  5. Net result: HCl in lumen, bicarbonate enters blood

Acid & Pepsinogen Stimulators

  • Parasympathetic nervous system (ACh)
  • Gastrin (from G cells)
  • Histamine (paracrine from ECL cells)

Phases of Gastric Secretion

  • Cephalic phase: Sight, taste, smell, chewing, swallowing → ↑ parasympathetic → ↑ gastrin & acid
  • Gastric phase: Proteins + distension in stomach → short/long reflexes → ↑ gastrin, acid, pepsinogen
  • Intestinal phase: Food in duodenum (acid, fat, osmolarity, distension) → INHIBITS gastric secretion

Pancreatic Juice Secretion

  • Acinar cells: Small volume, enzymes (stimulated by CCK)
  • Duct cells: Large volume, bicarbonate-rich (stimulated by secretin)
  • Enzyme and bicarbonate secretion regulated independently
  • Composition varies based on lumenal contents

Bile Secretion Regulation

  • Secretin: Stimulates bile secretion from liver
  • CCK:
    • Stimulates gallbladder contraction
    • Relaxes sphincter of Oddi

Peptic Ulcers

Most located in duodenum. Caused by H. pylori or NSAIDs.

Motility

GI Motility Overview

Movements of the GI wall due to contractions of the muscularis externa.

Function: Mix and propel GI tract contents

Slow Waves & Basic Electric Rhythm (BER)

  • Spontaneous slow waves of depolarization in smooth muscle
  • Frequency = Basic Electric Rhythm (BER)
  • BER varies in different areas of GI tract
  • Height of BER affected by neural and hormonal input:
    • Parasympathetic → Excitation (↑ depolarization)
    • Sympathetic → Inhibition (↓ depolarization)
  • Action potentials occur when threshold is reached → contraction

Electrical vs Mechanical Activity

  • Stomach: Larger depolarizations → stronger contractions; action potentials → even stronger
  • Intestines: Action potentials required for force; strength varies with AP frequency

PurposePropels food forwardMixes food
MechanismProximal: circular contracts, longitudinal relaxes (↓ diameter)
Distal: circular relaxes, longitudinal contracts (↑ diameter)		Alternating contractions between segments

Chewing Reflex

  • Control is both voluntary and involuntary
  • Cyclical reflex:
    1. Normally jaw muscles active (hold mouth closed)
    2. Food enters mouth → inhibits jaw muscles
    3. Jaw drops → relieves pressure → muscles contract
    4. Pressure from food restored → cycle repeats

Swallowing Reflex

  • Bolus = chewed food + saliva
  • Integration center: Swallowing center (medulla oblongata)
  • Sequence:
    1. Tongue moves bolus to pharynx
    2. Epiglottis covers glottis (prevents food entering trachea)
    3. Inspiration inhibited
    4. Upper esophageal sphincter relaxes
    5. Bolus enters esophagus; sphincter closes behind
    6. Bolus stretches esophagus → triggers peristalsis
    7. Lower esophageal sphincter relaxes
    8. Bolus enters stomach
  • Travel time: ~9 seconds
  • Receptive relaxation: Stomach smooth muscle relaxes to receive bolus

Gastric Motility

  • Functions: Mix chyme, regulate gastric emptying
  • Waves of peristalsis: upper body → pylorus (strengthen approaching pylorus)
  • Mixing: Pyloric sphincter closed
  • Emptying: Stronger contractions open pyloric sphincter

Gastric Motility Regulation

  • Increases force: Gastrin
  • Decreases force: CCK, Secretin, GIP
  • Phases:
    • Cephalic excitation: Anger, aggression
    • Cephalic inhibition: Pain, fear, depression
    • Gastric excitation: Distension of stomach
    • Intestinal inhibition: Distension/contents (osmolarity, acidity, fat) in duodenum

Migrating Motility Complex (MMC)

  • Wave of intense contractions
  • Travels short distances
  • Occurs between meals to clear stomach
  • Regulated by motilin

Small Intestine Motility Reflexes

  • Intestino-intestinal reflex: Injury/stress → inhibits intestinal contractions
  • Ileogastric reflex: Distension of ileum → inhibits gastric motility
  • Gastroileal reflex: Chyme in stomach → increases ileum motility

Colon Motility

  • Haustrations: Proximal colon; like segmentation but slower
  • Mass movement: Propels material toward rectum; similar to peristalsis but contraction lasts longer than relaxation

Colon Motility Reflexes

  • Colonocolonic reflex: Distension in one area → relaxation of other areas
  • Gastrocolic reflex: Food in stomach → increases colonic motility

Defecation

  • Voluntary and involuntary control
  • Distension activates stretch receptors
  • Smooth muscle of rectum contracts → ↑ rectal pressure
  • Peristaltic contractions of sigmoid colon push more material in
  • Internal anal sphincter: Smooth muscle - relaxes involuntarily
  • External anal sphincter: Skeletal muscle - contracts initially, then relaxes voluntarily
  • Both sphincters must relax for defecation

Vomiting

  • Stimuli: Illness, emotions, pain, GI tract distension, rotation of head, certain substances (emetics)
  • Reflex mediated through vomiting center in medulla
  • Protective reflex to expel toxins/pathogens

Diarrhea

Bacterial toxins enhance Cl- and water secretion into lumen → overwhelms absorption → watery stool

Special Topics

Vitamin B12 Absorption

Requires intrinsic factor (from parietal cells). Absorbed in ileum. Without IF → pernicious anemia.

Iron Absorption

  • DMT1: Apical transporter into enterocyte
  • Ferroportin: Basolateral transporter to blood
  • Hepcidin: ↓ absorption by blocking ferroportin

Cholesterol Absorption

Uses NPC1L1 transporter. Ezetimibe blocks this transporter.

Cystic Fibrosis

Defective CFTR chloride channel → thick pancreatic secretions → blocked ducts → maldigestion

M Cells & Gut Immunity

  • M cells: Specialized cells that sample antigens from gut lumen
  • Transfer antigens to immune cells in Peyer's patches
  • Blocking M cell receptors could prevent Salmonella infection

Intestinal Cell Junctions

Intestinal epithelial cells have tight junctions (not leaky junctions) - controls what passes between cells.

Key Takeaways

  • Four GI processes: Digestion, Absorption, Secretion, Motility
  • GI tract: 15 feet long; Small intestine: 8-10 feet; Stomach: 50-1000 mL
  • GI wall: Mucosa → Submucosa → Muscularis externa → Serosa
  • ENS: Meissner's (secretion) vs Auerbach's (motility)
  • Accessory glands: Salivary glands, Pancreas (acini), Liver, Gallbladder
  • Carbs (~500g/day): Mouth → Small intestine (amylase → brush border → monosaccharides)
  • Proteins (~125g/day): Stomach → Small intestine (pepsin → trypsin cascade → amino acids)
  • Fats (~50g/day): Small intestine only (bile emulsification → lipase → micelles → chylomicrons → lymph)
  • Enterohepatic circulation: Bile salts recycled (liver → gallbladder → duodenum → ileum → liver)
  • GI regulation is NOT homeostatic - maximizes absorption regardless of need
  • Hormones: Gastrin (HCl), CCK (bile/enzymes), Secretin (bicarb), GIP (insulin)
  • CCK & Secretin potentiate each other
  • Food intake: Leptin (satiety), Ghrelin (hunger), NPY/AgRP (orexigenic)
  • Phases: Cephalic (head), Gastric (stomach), Intestinal (small intestine)
  • Motility: Peristalsis (propulsion) vs Segmentation (mixing); BER sets rhythm
  • Swallowing reflex: ~9 seconds travel time; coordinated by medulla
  • Important reflexes: Gastrocolic, Ileogastric, Gastroileal, Colonocolonic
