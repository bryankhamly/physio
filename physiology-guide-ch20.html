Chapter 20

The Gastrointestinal System

Digestion, absorption, secretion, and motility

1

Overview

The Four GI Processes

  • Digestion: Break down food into absorbable particles
  • Absorption: Move nutrients from lumen → blood/lymph
  • Secretion: Release enzymes, mucus, acid, bile into lumen
  • Motility: Move contents through tract

Why Digestion?

Nutrients are too large to cross the epithelium. Must be broken down:

Starch→Glucose Proteins→Amino acids Triglycerides→Fatty acids + Monoglycerides
2

GI Anatomy

GI Tract Path

Mouth→ Pharynx→ Esophagus→ Stomach→ Small Intestine→ Colon→ Rectum

Four Layers of GI Wall (Lumen → Outside)

  1. Mucosa: Epithelium + Lamina propria (subepithelial connective tissue) + Muscularis mucosae (moves villi)
  2. Submucosa: Blood vessels, lymph vessels, nerve network (Meissner's plexus), Peyer's patches
  3. Muscularis externa: Inner circular + Outer longitudinal muscle, Myenteric plexus (Auerbach's) between layers
  4. Serosa: Outer connective tissue covering

Enteric Nervous System ("Second Brain")

  • Submucosal plexus (Meissner's): Controls secretion & blood flow
  • Myenteric plexus (Auerbach's): Controls motility

~100 million neurons - can function independently of CNS!

Surface Area Structures
Rugae → Plicae → Villi → Microvilli
GALT
80% of all lymphocytes!

Small Intestine Sections

Duodenum
Receives chyme, bile, pancreatic juice → Jejunum
Major nutrient absorption → Ileum
B12 & bile salt absorption

Hepatic Portal System

Absorbed nutrients go to liver first via hepatic portal vein, NOT directly to general circulation.

Large Intestine (Colon)

  • Absorbs water and electrolytes (NOT nutrients)
  • Produces and stores feces
  • Appendix: Located at ventral end of cecum
  • Flatus: Gas from bacterial fermentation of indigestible carbs

Key Structures

  • Mesenteries: Peritoneal sheets that hold intestines in place
  • Pylorus: Sphincter controlling stomach emptying into duodenum
  • Chyme: Processed stomach contents (food + gastric juice)
  • Intestinal crypts: Produce new cells AND increase surface area
3

Carbohydrate Digestion

Begins In
Mouth (salivary amylase)
Completed In
Small intestine

Key Carbohydrate Facts

  • Primary complex carb digested by humans: Starch
  • Amylase digests starch → disaccharides
  • Cellulose (fiber) CANNOT be digested by human enzymes

Digestion Steps

  1. Mouth: Salivary amylase → starch to maltose
  2. Stomach: Amylase inactivated (no carb digestion)
  3. Small intestine: Pancreatic amylase → disaccharides
  4. Brush border: Final enzymes → monosaccharides
EnzymeSubstrateProducts
MaltaseMaltoseGlucose + Glucose
SucraseSucroseGlucose + Fructose
LactaseLactoseGlucose + Galactose

Absorption

  • SGLT1: Na+-glucose symporter (apical) - glucose & galactose
  • GLUT5: Fructose transporter (apical)
  • GLUT2: All monosaccharides exit basolaterally

Lactose Intolerance

Lactase deficiency → undigested lactose fermented by bacteria → gas, bloating, diarrhea

4

Protein Digestion

Begins In
Stomach (pepsin)
Completed In
Small intestine

Digestion Steps

  1. Stomach: Pepsinogen → Pepsin (activated by HCl)
  2. Small intestine: Pancreatic proteases (trypsin, chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase)
  3. Brush border: Aminopeptidase removes amino acids

Activation Cascade

Enterokinase activates trypsinogen → trypsin → activates all other pancreatic proteases

TypeEnzymesAction
EndopeptidasesPepsin, Trypsin, ChymotrypsinCut in middle of protein
ExopeptidasesCarboxypeptidase, AminopeptidaseRemove from ends

Products & Absorption

Products: Free amino acids, dipeptides, tripeptides

Absorption via Na+-amino acid cotransporters and H+-peptide cotransporter (PepT1)

5

Fat Digestion

Location
Entirely in small intestine
Key Requirement
Bile emulsification

Digestion Steps

  1. Emulsification: Bile salts break fat into small droplets
  2. Digestion: Pancreatic lipase + colipase → monoglycerides + fatty acids
  3. Micelles: Transport products to brush border
  4. Absorption: Diffuse into enterocytes

Bile

  • Made by: Liver (hepatocytes)
  • Stored in: Gallbladder
  • Released by: CCK stimulus
  • Function: Emulsify fats (amphipathic)

Fat Absorption Pathway

Fatty acids enter cell → Reassembled to triglycerides → Packaged as chylomicrons → Enter LYMPH (lacteals)

Fats go to lymph, NOT blood directly!

Steatorrhea

Fat malabsorption → fatty stool. Causes: gallstones, pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis. Also causes fat-soluble vitamin (A, D, E, K) deficiency.

Regulation

Key Principle

GI regulation is NOT homeostatic - it maximizes absorption regardless of need

PhaseStimulusMechanism
CephalicSight, smell, taste of foodVagus nerve (parasympathetic)
GastricFood in stomach, distensionENS + vagus + gastrin
IntestinalChyme in small intestineENS + hormones (CCK, secretin)

Autonomic Control

  • Parasympathetic (vagus): ↑ motility, ↑ secretion, relaxes sphincters
  • Sympathetic: ↓ motility, ↓ secretion, contracts sphincters
Short Reflexes
Within ENS (enteric)
Long Reflexes
Involve CNS

Reflex Types

  • Feedforward reflexes = Cephalic reflexes (originate outside GI - sight, smell, emotions)
  • Long reflexes: Include feedforward + emotional reflexes

GI Hormones

HormoneSourceStimulusEffects
GastrinG cells (stomach)Peptides, distension↑ HCl, ↑ motility
CCKI cells (duodenum)Fats, amino acids↑ Bile, ↑ enzymes, ↓ emptying
SecretinS cells (duodenum)Acid↑ Bicarbonate, ↓ acid
GIPK cells (duodenum)Glucose, fats↑ Insulin release
MotilinSmall intestineFastingMMC (housekeeping)

Important Cells

  • Parietal cells: HCl + Intrinsic factor
  • Chief cells: Pepsinogen
  • ECL cells: Histamine (stimulates HCl)
  • D cells: Somatostatin (inhibits GI hormones)

CCK vs Secretin

  • CCK: Responds to FATS → releases ENZYMES + BILE
  • Secretin: Responds to ACID → releases BICARBONATE

Hormone-Stimulus Pairs (Know These!)

  • Gastrin - peptides/amino acids (CORRECT)
  • CCK - fatty foods (CORRECT)
  • Secretin - acid in small intestine (CORRECT)
  • GIP - glucose in small intestine (CORRECT)
  • Motilin - acid in stomach (WRONG! Motilin = fasting)
8

Secretion

Saliva

  • Functions: Lubrication, protection, starch digestion, antibacterial
  • Parasympathetic: Watery, enzyme-rich (primary control)
  • Sympathetic: Thick, mucus-rich
  • Parotid gland blockage: Less viscous saliva, ↓ carb digestion, ↓ lubrication, ↓ taste

Gastric Juice

  • HCl: Activates pepsin, kills bacteria, denatures proteins
  • Pepsinogen: Inactive → pepsin (by HCl)
  • Intrinsic factor: Required for B12 absorption
  • Mucus: Protects stomach lining

HCl Production

Carbonic anhydrase in parietal cells produces H+ for HCl. Blocking this enzyme → higher pH (less acid).

Stimulators: ACh (vagus) + Gastrin (G cells) + Histamine (ECL cells)

Pancreatic Juice

  • Bicarbonate (duct cells) - stimulated by secretin - neutralizes acid
  • Enzymes (acinar cells) - stimulated by CCK
  • Exocrine pancreas = lobules called acini that secrete digestive enzymes
  • Pancreas secretes more protein (enzymes) relative to weight than any other tissue!

Mucus Functions

Protection + Lubrication (NOT enzyme activation!)

Goblet cells secrete mucus throughout the GI tract.

Peptic Ulcers

Most located in duodenum. Caused by H. pylori or NSAIDs.

9

Motility

Slow Waves

Generated by Interstitial Cells of Cajal (pacemaker cells). Cycles of depolarization and repolarization that set maximum contraction frequency.

Contraction Types

  • Phasic contractions: Last only seconds, occur in stomach and small intestine
  • Tonic contractions: Sustained for minutes, occur in sphincters
TypePeristalsisSegmentation
PurposePropels food forwardMixes food
PatternWave moves aborallyAlternating contractions
LocationEntire GI tractMainly small intestine

Swallowing

  • Center: Medulla oblongata
  • Upper esophageal sphincter relaxes
  • Lower esophageal sphincter relaxes
  • Respiration inhibited

Gastric Emptying

Slowed by: Fat, acid, or hyperosmolar chyme in duodenum (via CCK, secretin)

Increased by: Gastrin

Migrating Motor Complex (MMC)

"Housekeeping" during fasting. Regulated by motilin. Sweeps debris and bacteria to colon.

Defecation

  • Rectal distension → spinal reflex
  • Internal anal sphincter: Smooth muscle - relaxes involuntarily
  • External anal sphincter: Skeletal muscle - consciously controlled
Gastrocolic Reflex
Food in stomach → ↑ colonic motility
Mass Movements
Push feces toward rectum

Diarrhea

Bacterial toxins enhance Cl- and water secretion into lumen → overwhelms absorption → watery stool

Vomiting

  • Protective reflex to expel toxins/pathogens
  • Similar to diarrhea: both rid the body of damaging agents
  • Coordinated by vomiting center in medulla

Special Topics

Vitamin B12 Absorption

Requires intrinsic factor (from parietal cells). Absorbed in ileum. Without IF → pernicious anemia.

Iron Absorption

  • DMT1: Apical transporter into enterocyte
  • Ferroportin: Basolateral transporter to blood
  • Hepcidin: ↓ absorption by blocking ferroportin

Cholesterol Absorption

Uses NPC1L1 transporter. Ezetimibe blocks this transporter.

Cystic Fibrosis

Defective CFTR chloride channel → thick pancreatic secretions → blocked ducts → maldigestion

M Cells & Gut Immunity

  • M cells: Specialized cells that sample antigens from gut lumen
  • Transfer antigens to immune cells in Peyer's patches
  • Blocking M cell receptors could prevent Salmonella infection

Intestinal Cell Junctions

Intestinal epithelial cells have tight junctions (not leaky junctions) - controls what passes between cells.

Key Takeaways

  • Four GI processes: Digestion, Absorption, Secretion, Motility
  • GI wall: Mucosa → Submucosa → Muscularis externa → Serosa
  • ENS: Meissner's (secretion) vs Auerbach's (motility)
  • Carbs: Mouth → Small intestine (amylase + brush border)
  • Proteins: Stomach → Small intestine (pepsin + pancreatic proteases)
  • Fats: Small intestine only (bile + lipase → micelles → chylomicrons → lymph)
  • Hormones: Gastrin (HCl), CCK (bile/enzymes), Secretin (bicarb), GIP (insulin)
  • Phases: Cephalic (head), Gastric (stomach), Intestinal (small intestine)
  • Motility: Peristalsis (propulsion) vs Segmentation (mixing)
