Chapter 20

The Gastrointestinal System

Digestion, absorption, secretion, and motility

1

Overview

The Four Core GI Functions

  • Digestion: Break large food molecules into absorbable units
  • Absorption: Move nutrients from lumen into blood or lymph
  • Secretion: Release enzymes, acid, mucus, and bile into the lumen
  • Motility: Propel and mix contents through the tract

Why Break Down Food?

Nutrients are too large to cross the intestinal epithelium. Digestion converts:

Starch→Glucose Proteins→Amino acids Triglycerides→Fatty acids + Monoglycerides
2

GI Anatomy

Total Tract Length
~15 feet
Small Intestine
8-10 feet

GI Tract Pathway

Mouth→ Pharynx→ Esophagus→ Stomach→ Small Intestine→ Colon→ Rectum

Four Layers of the GI Wall (Lumen → Out)

  1. Mucosa: Epithelium (absorption/secretion) + Lamina propria (blood vessels, immune cells) + Muscularis mucosae (local mixing)
  2. Submucosa: Connective tissue, blood vessels, lymphatics, and **Meissner’s plexus** (controls secretion)
  3. Muscularis externa: Inner circular + outer longitudinal muscle; **Auerbach’s plexus** between layers (controls motility)
  4. Serosa: Outer protective layer (visceral peritoneum)

Enteric Nervous System ("Second Brain")

  • Meissner’s plexus (submucosal): Controls secretion and blood flow
  • Auerbach’s plexus (myenteric): Controls motility

~100 million neurons—can function independently of the CNS!

Small Intestine Regions

Duodenum
Receives chyme, bile, pancreatic juice → Jejunum
Major site of nutrient absorption → Ileum
Absorbs B12 and bile salts

Key fact: Nutrient absorption is complete within the first 20% of the small intestine!

Surface Area Maximizers

  • Plicae circulares: Permanent circular folds
  • Villi: Finger-like projections with blood capillaries and a lacteal (lymph vessel)
  • Microvilli: Form the "brush border" on enterocytes

Hepatic Portal System

  • Nutrients absorbed into mesenteric veins
  • Travel via hepatic portal vein → liver FIRST
  • Liver processes nutrients before they enter systemic circulation

Large Intestine Functions

  • Absorb water and electrolytes
  • Form and store feces
  • Harbor gut microbiota (ferment fiber → short-chain fatty acids)
3

Accessory Glands

Salivary Glands

  • Secrete saliva: mucus (lubrication), amylase (starch digestion), lysozyme (antibacterial)

Pancreas

  • Exocrine: Acinar cells → digestive enzymes; duct cells → bicarbonate
  • Endocrine: Islets of Langerhans → insulin/glucagon

Liver & Gallbladder

  • Liver: Produces bile from cholesterol
  • Gallbladder: Stores and concentrates bile; releases it when CCK is stimulated
  • Enterohepatic circulation: 95% of bile salts reabsorbed in ileum and returned to liver
4

Carbohydrate Digestion

Digestion Pathway

  1. Mouth: Salivary amylase → starch → maltose
  2. Small intestine: Pancreatic amylase continues breakdown
  3. Brush border enzymes: Final digestion to monosaccharides

Absorption Mechanisms

  • Glucose & Galactose: SGLT1 (Na⁺-linked) → GLUT2 (basolateral)
  • Fructose: GLUT5 → GLUT2 (facilitated diffusion)

Lactose Intolerance

Lactase deficiency → undigested lactose → bacterial fermentation → gas, bloating, diarrhea.

5

Protein Digestion

Digestion Steps

  1. Stomach: HCl + pepsinogen → pepsin → peptides
  2. Small intestine: Trypsinogen → trypsin (via enterokinase)
  3. Trypsin activates: Chymotrypsin, carboxypeptidase

Absorption

  • Amino acids: Na⁺-linked or facilitated diffusion
  • Dipeptides/tripeptides: H⁺-linked cotransport → broken down inside cell
6

Fat Digestion

The Challenge

Fats are hydrophobic → form large droplets → lipases can only act at the surface.

Solution: Emulsification & Micelles

  1. Bile salts emulsify fats → tiny droplets
  2. Pancreatic lipase breaks them → fatty acids + monoglycerides
  3. Products form micelles → diffuse into enterocytes
  4. Inside cell: reassembled into triglycerides → packed into chylomicrons
  5. Chylomicrons enter lacteals (lymph) → not blood!

Steatorrhea

Fat malabsorption → oily stools. Causes: gallstones, pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis.

7

Vitamins & Minerals Absorption

Fat-Soluble Vitamins (A, D, E, K)

Absorbed with fats → enter lymph via chylomicrons.

Vitamin B12

Requires intrinsic factor (from parietal cells) → absorbed in ileum. Deficiency → pernicious anemia.

Water & Electrolytes

  • Water absorbed passively via osmosis (follows solutes)
  • Na⁺ actively absorbed → drives absorption of other nutrients
8

GI Regulation

Key Principle

GI regulation is NOT homeostatic—it maximizes nutrient absorption regardless of body needs.

Control Systems

  • Enteric Nervous System (ENS): Local reflexes (Meissner’s & Auerbach’s)
  • Autonomic: Parasympathetic (↑ activity), Sympathetic (↓ activity)

Hormonal Phases

  • Cephalic: Sight/smell/taste → vagus nerve
  • Gastric: Food in stomach → gastrin release
  • Intestinal: Chyme in duodenum → CCK, secretin
9

Food Intake Regulation

Leptin (Long-Term)

From fat cells → signals satiety → ↓ appetite, ↑ metabolism.

Ghrelin (Short-Term)

From empty stomach → "hunger hormone" → ↑ appetite.

Secretion

Gastric Acid Secretion

Parietal cells: CO₂ + H₂O → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ → H⁺ secreted via H⁺/K⁺ ATPase.

Pancreatic Juice

  • Acinar cells: Enzymes (stimulated by CCK)
  • Duct cells: Bicarbonate (stimulated by secretin)

Special Topics

Cystic Fibrosis

Defective CFTR Cl⁻ channel → thick mucus → blocked pancreatic ducts → maldigestion.

Iron Absorption

DMT1 (apical) → ferroportin (basolateral); regulated by hepcidin.

Key Takeaways

  • Four processes: Digestion, Absorption, Secretion, Motility
  • Small intestine = absorption superstar
  • Fats → lymph (via chylomicrons), NOT blood
  • Bile emulsifies fats — made in liver, stored in gallbladder
  • CCK & secretin work together
  • ENS = "second brain" with 100M neurons
  • GI regulation maximizes absorption — not homeostasis!
  • Nutrients go to liver FIRST via hepatic portal vein
