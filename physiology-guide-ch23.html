Chapter 23

The Immune System

Defense, recognition, and memory

1

Overview

Two Types of Immunity

  • Innate immunity: Nonspecific, present at birth, immediate response
  • Adaptive immunity: Specific, acquired, develops after exposure
First Line of Defense
Skin & mucous membranes
Lymphocyte Population
~1 trillion in adults

Key Distinction

  • Innate: Same response every time (no memory)
  • Adaptive: Faster & stronger on re-exposure (memory cells)

Four Basic Steps of Immune Response

  1. Detection: Recognize foreign invader
  2. Communication: Alert other immune cells
  3. Recruitment: Mobilize defenses to site
  4. Elimination: Destroy pathogen
2

Pathogens

Types of Pathogens

  • Bacteria: Single-celled prokaryotes (killed by antibiotics)
  • Viruses: Must reproduce inside host cells, parasitic
  • Fungi: Eukaryotes (e.g., athlete's foot)
  • Parasites: Multicellular pathogens (e.g., hookworms)

Antibiotics Target

Bacteria ONLY - NOT viruses, fungi, or parasites!

What is NOT a Pathogen?

Cancer - it's abnormal cell growth, not an invader. Also, not all bacteria are pathogens (gut microbiome is beneficial).

Virus Characteristics

  • Must reproduce inside host cells
  • Are parasitic
  • Cannot survive long outside the body
  • Not killed by antibiotics

Primary vs Secondary Lymphoid Organs

  • Primary: Where lymphocytes develop (bone marrow, thymus)
  • Secondary: Where immune responses occur (spleen, lymph nodes, GALT)

Swollen Lymph Nodes

Indicates immune cells are fighting infection - lymphocytes are proliferating in the affected nodes.

Immune Cells

Leukocytes (White Blood Cells)

All formed in bone marrow from hematopoietic stem cells

Cell TypeKey Facts
NeutrophilsMost abundant WBC, first responders, live only 6-12 hours, phagocytic
EosinophilsFight parasites & allergies, cytotoxic granules (NOT short-lived like neutrophils)
BasophilsRare in blood, have granules, related to mast cells
MonocytesPrecursors to macrophages (become macrophages in tissues)
MacrophagesLarge phagocytes, APCs, fixed in tissues
LymphocytesB cells, T cells, NK cells - adaptive immunity
NK cellsKill virus-infected & cancer cells, immune surveillance
Dendritic cellsBest APCs, Langerhans cells in skin
Mast cellsIn tissues, release histamine, trigger inflammation

Monocytes vs Macrophages

Monocytes are in blood → become Macrophages when they enter tissues

Mononuclear Phagocyte System

Includes only macrophages and monocytes (NOT neutrophils, eosinophils, or lymphocytes).

Tissue-Specific Macrophages

  • Microglia: Brain
  • Kupffer cells: Liver
  • Langerhans cells: Skin (dendritic cells)
  • Alveolar macrophages: Lungs

Antigen-Presenting Cells (APCs)

Present antigens to T cells via MHC molecules:

  • Dendritic cells - most effective APCs
  • Macrophages
  • B cells

NOT APCs: Neutrophils, lymphocytes (T cells)

Cell Counts Indicate Infection Type

  • ↑ Neutrophils + Macrophages: Bacterial infection
  • ↑ Eosinophils + Monocytes: Parasitic infection
  • ↑ Lymphocytes, Eosinophils, Basophils: Allergic reaction
  • ↑ Basophils: Inflammation

Memory Trick

  • Chemotaxins = Attract cells (chemotaxis)
  • Opsonins = Tag for eating (Greek: "to prepare food")
  • Pyrogens = Fire/Fever
  • Interferons = Interfere with viruses

Acute Phase Proteins

  • Produced by liver
  • C-reactive protein (CRP) is an acute phase protein
  • ↑ CRP indicates inflammation/infection

Complement System

  • ~30 plasma proteins (made by liver)
  • Cascade activation (like clotting)
  • Functions: Opsonization, inflammation, cell lysis (MAC)
  • Can activate without antibodies (alternative pathway)
6

Innate Immunity

Characteristics

  • Present at birth
  • Nonspecific - same response to all pathogens
  • No memory - same strength each time
  • Immediate response

Physical & Chemical Barriers

  • Skin: First line of defense
  • Mucous membranes: Trap pathogens
  • Stomach acid: Kills ingested pathogens
  • Lysozyme: In tears, saliva - kills bacteria

Pattern Recognition Receptors (PRRs)

  • Recognize pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs)
  • Toll-like receptors (TLRs) are a type of PRR
  • Found on macrophages, dendritic cells, neutrophils

Cell Movement

  • Chemotaxis: Movement toward chemical signals
  • Diapedesis: WBCs squeezing through blood vessel walls to reach tissues

Inflammation

Triggered when mast cells release histamine

Tissue damage → Mast cell degranulation → Histamine release → Vasodilation + ↑ permeability

Cardinal signs: Redness, Heat, Swelling, Pain

Phagocytosis

  • Phagocytes: Neutrophils, macrophages, dendritic cells
  • Initial defense = phagocytes (NOT plasma cells)
  • Engulf and digest pathogens in phagosomes

NK Cells - Immune Surveillance

  • Part of innate immunity
  • Kill virus-infected cells and cancer cells
  • Use perforin and granzymes
  • Cause apoptosis of target cells
7

Adaptive Immunity

Characteristics

  • Specific: Targets specific antigens
  • Memory: Faster response on re-exposure
  • Acquired: Develops after exposure
  • Slower: Takes days to develop
Humoral Immunity
B cells → Antibodies
Cell-Mediated Immunity
T cells → Direct killing

NOT Characteristic of Adaptive Immunity

Nonspecific immunity - that's innate immunity!

Antigens

  • Substances that trigger immune response
  • Must bind to specific receptors on lymphocyte membrane
  • Antigenic determinants (epitopes): Specific regions antibodies recognize

Clonal Expansion

Antigen binds lymphocyte → Activation → Proliferation → Effector cells + Memory cells

Clones = groups of lymphocytes that bind to a particular antigen (ligand).

Tolerance

Tolerance = when the immune system does NOT respond to a particular antigen (e.g., self-antigens on body cells). Loss of tolerance → autoimmune disease.

TypeDefinitionExample
Active immunityBody makes own antibodiesInfection, vaccination
Passive immunityReceive antibodies from another sourceBreast milk, antivenom

Active vs Passive

  • Vaccine: Active immunity (stimulates YOUR immune system)
  • Breast milk antibodies: Passive immunity
  • Antibodies through placenta (IgG): Passive immunity
8

Antibodies (Immunoglobulins)

Antibody Structure

  • 2 Heavy chains + 2 Light chains
  • Variable region: Antigen-binding site (Fab) - determines specificity
  • Constant region: Fc region - determines antibody class
  • Connected by disulfide bonds
  • Hinge region: Allows flexibility

Who Makes Antibodies?

Plasma cells (differentiated B cells) - NOT T cells, NOT B cells themselves!

Ig ClassKey Facts
IgG75% of blood antibodies, crosses placenta, secondary response
IgAIn secretions (tears, saliva, breast milk, mucus)
IgMFirst made (primary response), pentamer, reacts to blood group antigens
IgEAllergies, binds mast cells (triggers degranulation), fights parasites
IgDB cell receptor, regulates B cell activation

Immunoglobulin Memory Tricks

  • IgG = "G"oes through placenta, "G"reatest amount
  • IgA = "A"ll secretions
  • IgM = "M"ade first (pri"M"ary response)
  • IgE = all"E"rgies
  • IgD = on B cell surface (receptor)

High IgM Level Indicates

Person is currently fighting an infection (first antibody made). High IgG means past infection or vaccination.

Antibody Functions

  • Neutralization: Block pathogen binding sites
  • Opsonization: Tag for phagocytosis
  • Agglutination: Clump pathogens together
  • Complement activation: Trigger complement cascade

T Cells & MHC

T Cell Development

  • Originate in bone marrow
  • Mature in thymus
  • Responsible for cell-mediated immunity
T Cell TypeFunctionRecognizes
Helper T cells (CD4+)Coordinate immune response, activate other cellsMHC II
Cytotoxic T cells (CD8+)Kill infected/abnormal cells directlyMHC I
Regulatory T cellsSuppress immune response, prevent autoimmunity-

Helper T Cells are Central

A decrease in helper T cells would decrease ALL parts of immune response (they coordinate everything).

MHC (Major Histocompatibility Complex)

  • MHC Class I: On ALL nucleated cells, presents to CD8+ T cells
  • MHC Class II: Only on APCs (dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells), presents to CD4+ T cells

MHC I + Antigen → Cytotoxic T Cell Activation

When virus-infected cell displays foreign antigen on MHC I, cytotoxic T cells recognize and kill it using perforin and granzymes.

Cytotoxic T Cells vs NK Cells

  • Both kill virus-infected cells
  • Both use perforin and granzymes
  • NK cells: Innate (no prior exposure needed)
  • Cytotoxic T cells: Adaptive (specific, require activation)

Clinical Applications

Blood Types

  • Determined by glycoprotein antigens on RBC surface
  • Type A: A antigen, anti-B antibodies in plasma
  • Type B: B antigen, anti-A antibodies in plasma
  • Type AB: Both antigens, NO antibodies (universal recipient)
  • Type O: No antigens, both antibodies (universal donor)

Blood Type A Test Example

Blood agglutinates with anti-A but NOT anti-B = Type A

Person has anti-B antibodies in plasma, can receive A or O blood.

ConditionDescription
Allergy/HypersensitivityOveractive response to harmless antigen (IgE-mediated)
Autoimmune diseaseImmune system attacks self (incorrect response)
ImmunodeficiencyWeak/absent immune response

HIV/AIDS

  • HIV infects helper T cells (CD4+)
  • Destroys immune coordination
  • Leads to immunodeficiency (AIDS)
  • Opportunistic infections become deadly

Transplant Rejection

  • Result of cell-mediated immunity (T cells)
  • Cyclosporin A: Inhibits cytotoxic T cells to prevent rejection
  • T cells see transplanted organ as "foreign" (different MHC)

Stress and Immunity

Chronic stress → ↑ cortisol → immunosuppression → increased susceptibility to infection

Nervous-Endocrine-Immune Connection

Cytokines from immune system can affect neuroendocrine function - this is how the three systems communicate!

Key Takeaways

  • Innate = nonspecific, no memory; Adaptive = specific, has memory
  • First line of defense: skin and mucous membranes
  • Neutrophils = most abundant WBC, first responders
  • Monocytes → Macrophages when entering tissues
  • APCs: Dendritic cells, macrophages, B cells (NOT neutrophils)
  • Plasma cells make antibodies (not B cells directly)
  • IgG = most abundant, crosses placenta; IgM = first made; IgE = allergies; IgA = secretions
  • Helper T cells coordinate everything; Cytotoxic T cells kill infected cells
  • MHC I on all nucleated cells; MHC II only on APCs
  • HIV attacks helper T cells (CD4+)
