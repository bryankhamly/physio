The Immune System

How your body defends against invaders

1

Immune System Overview

The Two-Team Defense Strategy

  • Innate Immunity:
    • Always on duty
    • Same response every time
    • No memory
    • Acts within minutes
  • Adaptive Immunity:
    • Special forces
    • Tailored response
    • Creates memory
    • Acts within days
Immune Cells
~1 trillion in adults
Defense Strategy
Innate + Adaptive = Perfect Team

How Immunity Works: 4-Step Plan

  1. Detect: Recognize "non-self"
  2. Alert: Signal other immune cells
  3. Deploy: Send reinforcements to site
  4. Destroy: Eliminate the threat
2

First Line: Physical & Chemical Barriers

Your Body's Security System

Skin: Waterproof barrier with antimicrobial fatty acids → Mucous: Sticky trap for invaders → Cilia: Escalator that sweeps mucus out

Chemical Defenders

  • Lysozyme: In tears/saliva - breaks bacterial walls
  • Stomach acid: pH ~2 - kills most ingested pathogens
  • Vaginal acid: Prevents bacterial overgrowth

Why Barriers Matter

They stop 90% of potential infections before they even start! When barriers fail, innate immunity takes over.

3

Pathogen Types & Targets

Four Types of Invaders

  • Viruses: Hijack your cells to replicate
  • Bacteria: Single-celled; can make toxins
  • Fungi: Absorb nutrients; grow slowly
  • Parasites: Protozoa or worms; often need vectors

Key Distinction

  • Antibiotics work ONLY on bacteria
  • Viruses need antivirals
  • Fungi need antifungals
  • Parasites need antiparasitics

Virulence vs Pathogenicity

  • Pathogenicity: Ability to cause disease (yes/no)
  • Virulence: How severe the disease is (degree)

Immune Cell Factories

Central vs Peripheral Organs

  • Central (School):
    • Bone marrow: Makes ALL blood cells; B cells mature here
    • Thymus: T cells mature here
  • Peripheral (Battlefield):
    • Lymph nodes: Filter lymph
    • Spleen: Filters blood
    • MALT: Mucosal immune tissue (tonsils, Peyer's patches)
Bone Marrow
B cell maturation
Thymus
T cell maturation

Lymph Node Function

Lymph flows through nodes like a security checkpoint:

  1. Pathogens trapped by macrophages/dendritic cells
  2. Antigens presented to lymphocytes
  3. T and B cells activated → proliferation
  4. Cleaned lymph exits to continue circulation

Immune Cell Types

Stem Cell Lineages

Hematopoietic Stem Cell → Myeloid Line → Neutrophils, Monocytes, Eosinophils, Basophils
Hematopoietic Stem Cell → Lymphoid Line → B cells, T cells, NK cells

Phagocytes: The Clean-Up Crew

  • Neutrophils: First responders (70% of WBCs)
  • Macrophages: Big eaters; present antigens
  • Dendritic cells: Best antigen presenters

Lymphocytes: The Special Forces

  • B cells: Make antibodies
  • T cells: Direct killers (CD8+) or commanders (CD4+)
  • NK cells: Kill infected/cancer cells (innate)

Cell Count Clues

  • ↑ Neutrophils = Bacterial infection
  • ↑ Lymphocytes = Viral infection
  • ↑ Eosinophils = Parasite or allergy

Innate Immunity

Innate Recognition System

  • PAMPs: Pathogen patterns (e.g., bacterial LPS)
  • PRRs: Pattern recognition receptors on immune cells
  • TLRs: Toll-like receptors - key PRRs

Innate Components

  • Physical/chemical barriers
  • Phagocytes
  • Natural killer (NK) cells
  • Complement system
  • Inflammation & fever

NK Cells: Rapid Response

  • Kill virus-infected/cancer cells within hours
  • Use perforins to poke holes in targets
  • No memory - same response each time
7

Inflammation Explained

The 5-Step Inflammation Process

  1. Alarm: Macrophages detect pathogens → release cytokines
  2. Vasodilation: Histamine → more blood flow → redness/heat
  3. Increased permeability: Fluid leaks → swelling/pain
  4. Recruitment: Neutrophils arrive in 1 hour
  5. Cleanup: Phagocytosis eliminates invaders

Cardinal Signs = Histamine Effects

  • Rubor (Redness): Vasodilation
  • Calor (Heat): Increased blood flow
  • Tumor (Swelling): Fluid leakage
  • Dolor (Pain): Pressure + chemical signals

Phagocytosis in 4 Steps

  1. Chemotaxis: Move toward chemical signals
  2. Attachment: Bind to pathogen (often with opsonins)
  3. Engulfment: Form phagosome (takes 0.01 seconds!)
  4. Destruction: Phagosome + lysosome → digest

Opsonins: "Eat Me" Tags

  • Proteins that coat pathogens
  • Examples: Antibodies (IgG), Complement (C3b)
  • Make phagocytosis 1000x more efficient!

Macrophage Signals

  • IL-1: Activates T and B cells
  • IL-6: Causes fever, acute-phase proteins
  • TNF-α: Inflammation, can kill tumor cells

These act as endogenous pyrogens → signal hypothalamus → fever

Interferon Types

  • IFN-γ: From T cells/NK cells → boosts immunity

Complement System

Three Pathways, One Goal

  • Classical: Antibody-dependent (adaptive link)
  • Lectin: Mannose-binding on pathogens
  • Alternative: Spontaneous on bacterial surfaces

Complement Functions

Opsonization (C3b) → Chemotaxis (C5a) → Inflammation (C3a) → Lysis (MAC)

MAC Formation

Membrane Attack Complex = C5b-C9 → forms pore → osmotic lysis

10

Adaptive Immunity

Adaptive Immunity Features

  • Specificity: Targets exact antigen
  • Memory: Faster/stronger on re-exposure
  • Diversity: Recognizes millions of antigens
  • Self-tolerance: Doesn't attack self

Two Arms of Adaptive Immunity

  • Humoral: B cells → antibodies → extracellular threats
  • Cell-mediated: T cells → kill infected/cancer cells

Primary vs Secondary Response

  • Primary: 10-17 days to peak (symptoms occur)
  • Secondary: 2-7 days to peak (memory cells)

Clonal Selection

One antigen → selects specific lymphocyte → clones itself → effector + memory cells

Immunity Types

Active: Body makes own antibodies (infection/vaccine)✓ Memory
Passive: Receive ready-made antibodies (maternal, antivenom)✗ No memory

Antibodies Deep Dive

Antibody Structure

  • Y-shaped: 2 heavy + 2 light chains
  • Variable region: Antigen binding (tip of Y)
  • Constant region: Determines class/function (stem of Y)

Five Antibody Classes

IgGMost abundant; crosses placenta; secondary response
IgMFirst made; pentamer; primary response; activates complement
IgAMucosal immunity; breast milk
IgEAllergies; parasites
IgDB cell receptor

Memory Tricks

  • IgG = "G"oes through placenta
  • IgM = "M"ade first
  • IgA = "A"ll secretions
  • IgE = all"E"rgies

Antibody Actions

  • Neutralization: Block pathogen binding
  • Opsonization: Tag for phagocytosis
  • Agglutination: Clump pathogens together
  • Complement activation: IgM/IgG trigger classical pathway

T Cells & MHC

T Cell Types

  • Helper T (CD4+): Immune system "generals"
  • Cytotoxic T (CD8+): "Assassins" that kill infected cells
  • Regulatory T: Suppress immune response (prevent autoimmunity)

MHC: The ID Card System

MHC Class IAll nucleated cells → show internal problems → CD8+ T cells
MHC Class IIAPCs only → show external threats → CD4+ T cells

HLA = Human MHC

Unique to each person → causes organ rejection if mismatched

T Cell Activation

  1. APC presents antigen on MHC II to CD4+ T cell
  2. APC secretes IL-1 → T cell proliferates
  3. Helper T cell secretes IL-2 → activates B cells, CD8+ T cells, macrophages

Cytotoxic T Cell Killing

  • Binds to antigen on MHC I
  • Releases perforins → pores in target
  • Releases granzymes → trigger apoptosis

Clinical Connections

Blood Types

  • Type O: Universal donor (no antigens)
  • Type AB: Universal recipient (no antibodies)

Transplant Rejection

  • Caused by HLA mismatch
  • Treated with immunosuppressants (cyclosporine)

Allergy Mechanism

  1. Allergen → IgE production
  2. IgE binds mast cells
  3. Re-exposure → mast cell degranulation → histamine release

Anaphylaxis

  • Systemic histamine release
  • Vasodilation → ↓ blood pressure
  • Treatment: Epinephrine (vasoconstriction)

Autoimmune Diseases

  • Failure of self-tolerance
  • Examples: Type 1 diabetes, MS, lupus, RA

Immunodeficiency

  • AIDS: HIV destroys CD4+ T cells
  • SCID: "Bubble boy" disease

Stress and Immunity

  • Cortisol suppresses immune function
  • ↓ Lymphocyte proliferation
  • ↓ Inflammatory response

Key Takeaways

  • Innate = immediate, nonspecific; Adaptive = delayed, specific with memory
  • Barriers stop 90% of infections
  • Phagocytes: neutrophils (first), macrophages (sustained), dendritic cells (APCs)
  • Inflammation: redness, heat, swelling, pain from histamine
  • Opsonins (IgG, C3b) tag pathogens for phagocytosis
  • Macrophage cytokines (IL-1, IL-6, TNF-α) cause fever via hypothalamus
  • Complement: 3 pathways → opsonization, chemotaxis, inflammation, lysis
  • Primary response: 10-17 days; Secondary: 2-7 days (memory cells)
  • IgG = most abundant, crosses placenta; IgM = first made; IgE = allergies
  • MHC I (all cells) → CD8+ T cells; MHC II (APCs) → CD4+ T cells
  • Helper T cells (CD4+) coordinate entire immune response
  • Anaphylaxis: treat with epinephrine (vasoconstriction)
  • AIDS: HIV destroys CD4+ T cells → loss of immune coordination
