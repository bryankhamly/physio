Chapter 22

The Reproductive System

How gametes are made, hormones coordinate cycles, and new life begins

1

Overview: The Big Picture

Two Goals of Reproduction

  • Make gametes: Haploid sperm and eggs (each with 23 chromosomes)
  • Combine gametes: Form a diploid zygote (46 chromosomes) → embryo → baby
Autosomes
22 pairs (44 total)
Sex Chromosomes
1 pair: XX (female) or XY (male)

Key Terms Made Simple

  • Gamete: Mature sperm or egg (haploid, n=23)
  • Zygote: Fertilized egg (diploid, 2n=46)
  • Gestation: Pregnancy (~40 weeks)
  • Parturition: Birth!
2

Gametes & Meiosis: Making Haploid Cells

Why Meiosis?

If gametes were diploid like regular cells, the zygote would have 92 chromosomes! Meiosis halves the chromosome number so fertilization restores 46.

Meiosis in 2 Steps

1 Diploid cell (46 chromosomes) → Meiosis I: Homologous chromosomes separate → 2 Haploid cells (23 chromosomes, each with 2 chromatids) → Meiosis II: Sister chromatids separate → 4 Haploid gametes (23 single chromosomes)

Genetic Diversity Boosters

  • Crossing over: Homologous chromosomes swap segments in Prophase I
  • Independent assortment: Random alignment in Metaphase I → 8 million+ combos!
3

Sex Determination: It's All About the Y

The srY Gene is the Master Switch

  • srY gene on Y chromosome → makes testis-determining factor (TDF)
  • With TDF: Bipotential gonads become testes
  • Without TDF: Bipotential gonads become ovaries

Female = Default Pathway

No signal needed for female development. Male development requires active hormones from testes:

Testes SecreteEffect
Testosterone→ Wolffian ducts → Male structures (epididymis, vas deferens)
MIH→ Mullerian ducts degenerate

No testes? Wolffian ducts disappear, Mullerian ducts become female structures (uterus, tubes).

Male Hormonal Control: Steady as She Goes

The Feedback Loop

Hypothalamus → GnRH → Pituitary → FSH + LH
LH → Leydig cells → Testosterone
FSH → Sertoli cells → Spermatogenesis

Negative Feedback Keeps Balance

  • Testosterone → brain → ↓ GnRH/LH
  • Inhibin (from Sertoli cells) → pituitary → ↓ FSH only

Result: Stable sperm and testosterone production!

6

Spermatogenesis: From Stem Cell to Sperm

Sperm Parts & Functions

  • Head: Nucleus (23 chromosomes) + acrosome (enzymes for egg penetration)
  • Midpiece: Mitochondria (energy for tail)
  • Tail: Propels sperm with whip-like motion

Step-by-Step Production

  1. Spermatogonium (2n): Stem cell on basement membrane
  2. Mitosis: One stays as stem cell; one becomes primary spermatocyte
  3. Meiosis I: Primary spermatocyte → 2 secondary spermatocytes (n, but 2 chromatids)
  4. Meiosis II: → 4 spermatids (n)
  5. Spermiogenesis: Spermatids → mature sperm (remodeling takes ~64 days)

Sperm Maturation Timeline

  • 0-20 days: In seminiferous tubules → immotile
  • 20-60 days: In epididymis → gain motility
  • 60+ days: In vas deferens → stored until ejaculation

Female Anatomy: Cyclic System for One Egg at a Time

Ovaries: Egg & Hormone Center

  • Follicles: Each contains 1 immature egg (oocyte)
  • Granulosa cells: Surround oocyte; make estrogen
  • Theca cells: Outer layer; make testosterone (converted to estrogen by granulosa)

Uterine Layers

LayerFunction
Endometrium (inner)Sheds monthly; where embryo implants
Myometrium (middle)Thick smooth muscle → powerful contractions during labor
Perimetrium (outer)Protective serosa

Uterine Tubes: Egg's Highway

  • Fimbriae: Finger-like projections that "sweep" egg into tube
  • Cilia: Move egg toward uterus (takes ~4 days)
  • Fertilization site: Usually in ampulla (widest part)
9

Oogenesis: A Life-Long Egg Journey

Key Differences from Spermatogenesis

  • Fixed egg supply: All oogonia formed before birth
  • Meiosis pauses: Stops in Prophase I until puberty
  • Meiosis finishes only after fertilization!
  • 1 egg per cycle: Polar bodies get discarded

Oogenesis Timeline

Fetal Life: Oogonia → Primary oocytes → Meiotic arrest (Prophase I)

Puberty to Menopause: Each month, 1 primary oocyte resumes meiosis → completes Meiosis I → Secondary oocyte + 1st polar body

After Fertilization: Secondary oocyte completes Meiosis II → Ovum + 2nd polar body

Why Polar Bodies?

They discard extra chromosomes while conserving cytoplasm/nutrients for the egg. Only the mature ovum gets the resources!

Follicular1-14Menstrual → ProliferativeEstrogen ↑

Follicular Phase (Days 1-14)

  • Few follicles start developing → 1 becomes dominant
  • Granulosa cells produce estrogen
  • Estrogen rebuilds endometrium (proliferative phase)
  • High estrogen → triggers LH surge → ovulation

Luteal Phase (Days 15-28)

  • Ruptured follicle → corpus luteum
  • Corpus luteum makes progesterone + estrogen
  • Progesterone prepares endometrium for implantation (secretory phase)
  • No pregnancy? Corpus luteum degenerates → hormones drop → menstruation
  • Pregnancy? hCG saves corpus luteum!
11

Female Hormonal Control: The Estrogen Flip

The Two-Cell Model of Estrogen Production

LH → Theca cells → Testosterone → Testosterone → Granulosa cells → Estrogen

Both cell types must work together!

Estrogen's Dual Role

  • Low/Moderate levels: ↓ GnRH/LH/FSH (negative feedback)
  • High levels (late follicular): ↑ GnRH/LH (positive feedback → LH surge!)

Progesterone's Role

  • From corpus luteum after ovulation
  • Prepares endometrium for implantation
  • Suppresses uterine contractions (prevents miscarriage)
  • Blocks new ovulation (via strong negative feedback)
12

Fertilization: Race to the Egg

Sperm Survival
Up to 5 days
Egg Survival
12-24 hours

Sperm's Journey

  1. Ejaculated into vagina
  2. Swim through cervix → uterus → uterine tubes
  3. Undergo capacitation (activation in female tract)

Fertilization Steps

  1. Sperm bind to corona radiata (egg's outer layer)
  2. Acrosome reaction → enzymes digest zona pellucida
  3. First sperm fuses with egg membrane
  4. Cortical reaction: Zona hardens → blocks polyspermy!
  5. Egg completes meiosis II
  6. Sperm + egg pronuclei fuse → zygote
13

Pregnancy: From Zygote to Fetus

First Week Timeline

Day 1: Fertilization → Day 3: Morula (solid ball) → Day 5: Blastocyst (hollow ball) → Day 6-7: Implantation

Key Structures

  • Blastocyst:
    • Trophoblast: Becomes placenta
    • Inner cell mass: Becomes embryo
  • Placenta: Fetal + maternal tissue; exchanges gases/nutrients/wastes
  • hCG: Pregnancy hormone; detected by tests; saves corpus luteum

Hormonal Handoff

  • Weeks 1-8: Corpus luteum makes progesterone/estrogen
  • Week 8+: Placenta takes over hormone production
14

Birth & Breastfeeding: The Grand Finale

Parturition = Oxytocin Positive Feedback

Uterine contractions → Cervix stretches → Oxytocin release → Stronger contractions → Baby born!

Lactation Hormones

  • Prolactin: Makes milk (stimulated by suckling)
  • Oxytocin: Ejects milk ("let-down reflex")

Colostrum vs Mature Milk

  • Colostrum (days 1-5): Antibody-rich; protects newborn
  • Mature milk: Nutrient-rich; supports growth

Key Takeaways

  • Meiosis makes haploid gametes; fertilization restores diploid number
  • srY gene = male switch; female is default pathway
  • Male: Steady testosterone/sperm via negative feedback
  • Female: Cyclic changes driven by estrogen (flip from negative → positive feedback)
  • Follicular phase = egg maturation + estrogen rise → LH surge → ovulation
  • Luteal phase = corpus luteum makes progesterone → prepares for pregnancy
  • Fertilization requires capacitated sperm + blocks polyspermy
  • hCG rescues corpus luteum in early pregnancy
  • Birth = oxytocin positive feedback loop
  • Lactation: prolactin (milk synthesis) + oxytocin (milk ejection)
